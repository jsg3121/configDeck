Search Engine Journal reported on Similarweb's 2026 Generative AI Landscape report, which quantifies how often ChatGPT answers include outbound links, broken down by topic. The data covers U.S. desktop usage through May 2026.

## What's actually new

Travel queries receive citations in 22.6% of ChatGPT answers — more than three times the 6.8% average across all topics. Retail follows at 13.5%, then sports at 10.7%. Education sits at the bottom at 4.8%. The overall citation rate rose from about 1.3% in June 2025 to 6.8% in May 2026, though the trajectory wasn't linear — it spiked above 6% in October, dipped to around 4.5% by February, and climbed again through spring.

Beyond frequency, the *type* of site cited varies sharply. Reviews and UGC account for 54.1% of travel citations, while retail and e-commerce sites dominate beauty at 54.7%. In finance, finance-specific sites hold 36.6%. The data is drawn from what Similarweb labels the top 10,000 citations on U.S. desktop in May, and the company's own disclaimer notes these are estimates, not precise measurements.

## What it means for your config

This isn't a developer tooling announcement, so there are no config files, migration paths, or breaking changes to worry about. There's no SDK update, no API change, no new build flag.

That said, if you maintain tooling that tracks referral sources, monitors AI-generated traffic, or feeds into SEO dashboards, this data is worth noting as context. The citation rate is volatile — it swung by several percentage points across just a few months — so any automated alerting thresholds you've set around AI referral traffic should account for that instability rather than treating a single month's snapshot as a baseline. The report also doesn't cover API usage or mobile apps, meaning any internal analytics pipeline comparing your own ChatGPT-sourced traffic to Similarweb's numbers is comparing different populations.

## Recommended next step

If you're in a vertical where AI visibility matters (travel, retail, finance), read the full SEJ piece for the per-topic citation breakdowns and source-type splits. Don't over-index on a single month of data from a single vendor that sells AI visibility products — but do use it as a prompt to check your own analytics for trends in AI-referred sessions. The more useful signal here isn't any one number; it's that citation behavior differs enough by topic that a one-size-fits-all SEO or content strategy for AI engines probably won't hold up.
