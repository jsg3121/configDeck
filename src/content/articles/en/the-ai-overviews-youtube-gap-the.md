Search Engine Journal's Greg Jarboe makes the case that YouTube has become a primary citation source inside Google's AI Overviews, and that marketing teams who treated video as a side channel for 20 years are now paying for it. The article draws on YouTube's 2025 U.S. Impact Report and Jarboe's own two-decade experience with video-driven traffic strategies.

## What's actually new

The core data point comes from YouTube CEO Neal Mohan's post and Oxford Economics research: YouTube's creator ecosystem contributed more than $60 billion to U.S. GDP and supported over 540,000 full-time equivalent jobs. A separate YouTube blog post noted that 76% of small- and medium-sized businesses with a YouTube channel say the platform helped them grow their customer base. Jarboe's editorial argument layers on top of these numbers: YouTube videos are appearing inside AI Overviews with increasing frequency as cited answers, not just supplementary links. His prescription is to skip building in-house YouTube from zero and instead partner with existing creators who already have niche authority and production capability — then fold those partnerships into SEO and content workflows rather than siloing them in a separate influencer budget.

## Recommended next step

If you're on a team that manages SEO tooling or content pipelines, the actionable takeaway is narrower than the article's broad thesis: start tracking whether your domain's target queries are returning YouTube results inside AI Overviews. Tools like search scraping APIs or manual spot checks can tell you how often video is displacing text citations in your category. That data will tell you whether creator partnerships deserve engineering time for structured video embedding, updated sitemaps, or schema markup — or whether this is still a "marketing deck" conversation that hasn't hit your codebase yet. Don't reorganize your content infrastructure based on a trend piece; reorganize it based on your own SERP data.
